## Questions for Dr Kim first

1. **Your name in Korean.** The pages say "Mintae Kim 원장" because we don't know the Hangul spelling. Please send it (for example 김민태) and whether you prefer 원장, 원장님 or 박사.
2. **Slogans.** "slow is fast" is 느림이 곧 빠름, and "slowly is the fastest way…" is translated in full. Keep these, or leave the slogans in English as a brand line?
3. **Brand pillars.** Move / Build / Go is 움직이기 / 다지기 / 나아가기; Body / Mind / Life is 몸 / 마음 / 생활. Keep them in Korean, or in English?
4. **"Allied health"** is 연합보건(allied health). Accurate but uncommon with patients; 보건의료 전문직 is the alternative.
5. **Quoted definitions** (the IASP pain definition, the Ardern return-to-sport quote) are our own 해요체 renderings, not official Korean versions. Prefer the formal register or an established translation?
6. **"Why are you expensive?"** is 왜 비싼가요? with 저희가 가장 저렴한 곳은 아니에요. Please check the tone.
7. **"(see above)"** in the referral answer points to the Medicare answer, which actually sits further down the page, in both languages. Should the English say "(see below)"?
8. **Aged care** is 노인 치료 / 노인 재활 in the keyword band and 노인 요양(aged care) in the FAQ. Which do Korean families use?
9. **Muscle names.** The thumb muscles use the newer Korean anatomy names (긴엄지벌림근, 짧은엄지폄근), but gluteus medius uses the familiar 중둔근 rather than the newer 중간볼기근, because patients know it from gyms and Pilates. Keep this mix, or use one naming style throughout?
10. **Dry needling and acupuncture.** The post says acupuncture is "based on traditional Chinese medicine", translated as 중국 전통 의학에 바탕을 둔 침술 (not 한의학). Dry needling versus 침 is a sensitive topic for Korean readers. Is this wording right for them?
